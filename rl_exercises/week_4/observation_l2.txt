Using 5 seeds(0,1,2,3,4) changed the interpretation of the result. 
A single run can look very good or very bad depending on the random seed. 
With 5 seeds, the training curve gives a more reliable picture of the DQN agent's performance.

The median, IQM and mean are close to each other(around 0.75–0.76), 
which suggests that the final performance is not dominated by a single extreme seed. 
However, the confidence intervals are still relatively wide, meaning that the DQN result remains sensitive to random seeds. 
The optimality gap is around 0.24, which means that the agent is still about 24% away from the chosen target performance(250).

I feel more confident in the results than with a single seed, 
because the plot shows whether the learning trend is consistent across different random initializations. 
However, five seeds may still not enough, so large confidence intervals would indicate that the result is still uncertain.

In Level 1, I trained DQN on CartPole-v1 with fixed environment-step evaluation. 
I compared low, medium and large numbers of seeds.Because episode lengths differ across seeds, 
evaluating by episode can compare runs after different numbers of environment interactions. 
Fixed-step evaluation ensures all seeds are compared at the same training progress, so the statistics are aligned and fair.

The number of seeds has a strong influence on the interpretation. 
With only 3 seeds, the mean curve is very sensitive to individual runs and different low seed sets lead to visibly different conclusions. 
Some low seed sets suggest later improvement, while others suggest stagnation. This shows that conclusions based on very few seeds are unreliable.

The standard deviation does not always become smaller with more seeds, because it measures the variability of the algorithm itself. With more seeds, more diverse runs can be observed. 
In contrast, the standard error and the 95% confidence interval width generally become smaller when using more seeds. 
This means that the estimate of the mean performance becomes more reliable.

The comparison between mean, median and IQM shows that the mean can be influenced by outlier runs. 
In several parts of training, the mean is higher than the median and IQM, which suggests that a few strong runs pull the mean upward. 
Median and IQM are more robust and give a more conservative estimate of typical performance.
For low n=3, IQM is not very informative because the number of runs is too small. 
In my implementation, IQM is almost identical to the mean, so the two curves overlap.

Overall, the experiment shows that RL evaluation is highly sensitive to seed choice and aggregation metrics. 
Using more seeds and reporting robust metrics such as median or IQM gives a more reliable interpretation than only reporting the mean over a small number of seeds.

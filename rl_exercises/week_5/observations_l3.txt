I compared DDPG and continuous REINFORCE on Pendulum-v1 using the same environment, 
number of episodes (200), maximum trajectory length (200), discount factor (0.99), and random seed (0).

I used Pendulum-v1 because DDPG is designed for continuous action spaces. 
In this environment, the agent has to output a continuous torque value instead of choosing from a small set of discrete actions. 
Therefore, Pendulum-v1 is a simple and suitable test case for DDPG.

For a fair comparison, I used a continuous version of REINFORCE. 
This was necessary because the original policy_gradient.py implementation was designed for discrete action spaces.

The results show that DDPG learns much faster and achieves substantially better returns than REINFORCE. 
DDPG improves from around -1300 to approximately -200 episode return, and tends to become more stable over training.
In contrast, REINFORCE stays mostly between -1200 and -1400 and does not show a clear improvement.

The reason is that DDPG combines deterministic policy gradients with stabilizing ideas from DQN, especially replay buffers and target networks. 
The replay buffer allows DDPG to reuse past transitions and reduces correlations between samples. 
The target networks make the Bellman targets change more slowly, which stabilizes critic learning.

In contrast, REINFORCE is an on-policy Monte Carlo method. 
It updates the policy only after complete trajectories, so its gradient estimates will have higher variance, especially in continuous control tasks.
